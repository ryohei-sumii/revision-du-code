### 2. verdict = **revise**  (recall: improves / precision: neutral / already_covered: False)

### 4. verdict = **revise**  (recall: improves / precision: neutral / already_covered: False)
- **反論(最強の攻撃)**: The proposal asks the reviewer to Grep every symbol whose signature/return/nullability/units/errors/side-effects changed and read its call sites. This directly collides with two explicit anchors in the skill: Section 3's "Focus on the changed lines and what they touch; do not review the whole codebase" and the Gotcha "Respect the diff's scope. Pre-existing issues outside the changed lines are at most a brief aside, not the review." For a widely-used utility function (a rename of an internal helper, a changed error type on a core parser), "grep for call sites and read them" can mean opening dozens or hundreds of files across the repo — a categorically different (and unbounded) amount of work than "open the surrounding code," and squarely the kind of full-codebase archaeology the skill was written to prevent. Worse, plain-text Grep for a symbol name is an unreliable way to find real call sites in the first place: it misses dynamic dispatch, interfaces, decorators, reflection, and string-built calls, while over-matching common names (`get`, `run`, `close`, `Add`) with unrelated hits. So the instruction pays a real, sometimes large cost (time/tokens reading files outside the diff) for a recall gain that is inconsistent and, on the exact functions where the risk is highest (widely-shared APIs), likely to produce either an incomplete skim reported with false confidence or a review that balloons past "high-signal" into a sprawling caller audit — the opposite of the skill's stated goal.
- **反証への応答**: The scope objection proves too much: severity-rubric.md already says "Pre-existing issues outside the diff are capped at Low unless the change makes them materially worse" — a caller broken by a signature change is not a pre-existing issue, it is a new bug the diff introduces, explicitly carved out of that cap and eligible for Critical/High. And review-checklist.md's "Data & API contracts" bullet ("Breaking change to a public signature...") already tells the reviewer this category matters; what's missing is not permission to look, it's a method. Per the task's own premise, models follow concrete, literal instructions far more reliably than a mood-setting sentence ("hides its callers") that never says to act. Today Section 2 frames caller-reading purely as a false-positive guard ("Most false-positive review comments come from judging a hunk without reading the function it lives in") — there is no sentence anywhere that says go find the callers, only read the function's own body. That is a real, literal gap, and it is exactly the kind a model will not fill in by vibes. The scope-explosion risk is legitimate but fixable, not disqualifying: gate the instruction narrowly (only contract-shaped changes, which are a minority of hunks) and add an explicit proportionality valve — sample representative callers instead of exhaustively reading every hit when a symbol is used pervasively. That keeps the check inside "what the changed lines touch" (bounded, diff-driven) rather than "the whole codebase" (unbounded, audit-driven), while still catching the common, realistic case: a signature change with a handful to a few dozen callers, which is where this bug pattern actually lives and where a senior engineer would expect a reviewer to have checked before approving.
- **最終案**: In SKILL.md Section 2 ("Understand before judging"), after the existing paragraph, add:

"When a change alters a function or method's signature, return value/type, nullability, units, thrown/rejected errors, or side effects, Grep for its call sites and read the ones that still use the old contract — an unchanged caller relying on it is a bug the diff introduced, not a pre-existing issue (see severity-rubric.md's "materially worse" carve-out), and belongs in the review at full severity. If the symbol is used pervasively (a core utility, a widely-implemented interface), sample a representative few callers across different modules rather than reading every occurrence — the goal is to catch the realistic break, not to audit the whole codebase."

Optionally also tighten the checklist bullet in review-checklist.md under "Data & API contracts" from "Breaking change to a public signature, response shape, or DB schema." to "Breaking change to a public signature, response shape, or DB schema — grep its call sites/consumers and confirm they still hold under the new contract."
